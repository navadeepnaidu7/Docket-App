# High-end visual design

High visual quality comes from coherent decisions that serve the product. A particular font, layout, effect, or amount of decoration is not evidence of quality.

## Establish the direction

Read the brief, repository guidance, and the relevant screen's components and tokens. Existing product decisions and the user's references outrank this skill. Identify the platform and primary task before choosing a treatment. Use bundled, licensed, actually available assets and fonts; never assume an uninstalled premium typeface exists.

For an existing interface, extend its identity. Reuse its type roles, spacing scale, surfaces, controls, and motion owners. For a new interface or requested redesign, choose a coherent direction from the brief and suitable references. Do not randomize an archetype or vary an established layout merely to make it different.

## Make deliberate choices

- Typography: prioritize readable roles, appropriate line lengths, intentional weight and spacing, and clear hierarchy. Existing Inter, Roboto, Helvetica, system faces, or Material icons are valid when they fit the product. Do not replace them just to satisfy a stylistic ban.
- Composition: organize around the user's task and information needs. Compact lists, regular grids, conventional navigation, and restrained spacing can be the right solution. Large gaps, asymmetry, and overlapping cards need a reason.
- Materials: use color, borders, shadows, texture, and depth consistently with the local system. Add glass, gradients, nested enclosures, or grain only when the direction calls for them and they improve the actual surface. Flat surfaces are valid.
- Controls: preserve the product's established shape and variants. Pills and nested arrow circles are options, not requirements. Cover relevant loading, disabled, error, and success states without inventing unnecessary UI.
- Motion: use the product's motion system to explain state, provide feedback, or preserve continuity. Static content and instant updates are valid. Do not animate every element, add compulsory entrance sequences, or replace correct easing curves for novelty. Respect reduced motion.
- Platform: use web patterns for web, native conventions for native apps, and the actual project's responsive behavior. Do not impose one breakpoint, section padding, or CSS framework on every project.

## Check the actual result

When rendering is available and permitted by the task, inspect the affected surface at representative sizes and content states. Check hierarchy, wrapping, alignment, media, density, control states, and visual continuity with adjacent UI. Fix observed problems within scope. If only source is available, disclose that limit and avoid claiming the result looks polished or pixel-perfect.

For small refinements, prefer a focused change that improves the task over a collection of decorative alterations. Explain what changed, why it belongs in this product, and what was verified. Do not use a price tag, award label, or aesthetic checklist as the finish criterion.
